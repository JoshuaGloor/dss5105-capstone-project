LOOK AND SOUND
- Same palette and type as the deck: ink 1D1B18 background, ivory F4EFE6 text, camel A3804C
  accent; Georgia for titles, Arial for captions.
- Title cards TC1 to TC6: one line of ivory Georgia, centred on ink, nothing else on screen. Fade in
  over 6 frames, hold, hard cut out. Make them as text on a solid ink clip in the editor.
- Every shot moves. Slides: a slow push-in from 100% to 106% across the shot. When the voice names a
  part of a slide, PUNCH IN: cut to a 150-180% crop of that part. App: record the whole window,
  crop to the phone in the edit, and keyframe slow zooms. No aimless pointer movement.
- Hard cuts on the music beat. No wipes, spins or star effects. One dissolve, into the end card.
- Captions for every spoken line, bottom centre, Arial, white with a soft shadow. Numbers in
  captions must match this script exactly.
- Music: one instrumental track from the YouTube Audio Library (no attribution needed, so no
  copyright claim on upload). Minimal electronic or piano, about 100-110 BPM. It enters on TC3,
  sits about -22 dB under voices, rises to -14 dB on title cards, and drops out under the app's
  own spoken answers.
- App voice: full level only where this script says LET IT PLAY; elsewhere mute it in the edit.
- Voices: each speaker records alone in a quiet room, phone about 15 cm from the mouth (Voice Memos
  is fine). Read slower than feels natural, and pause half a beat after every number.
- Honesty: you may trim the model's wait, but keep at least one second of "Checking every
  workshop", and show the caption "Wait shortened" the first time you trim. Never speed up or
  change the app's answers.

Q&A PREPARATION (not read aloud)
- Ruby (Duong Thanh Nhan) cannot attend: Ruby emails Salman and Jack with the reason before the
  session, and slide 1 shows "Absent" with that reason. Any member may be asked about Ruby's desk
  redesign: one answer at a time, objective panel, saved plan, Show working.
- Who is Ravi? One of the five people in the course's dispatch chat (R02, R07, R12, R17, R22,
  R27). We made the person who posts requests our user and gave them the phone, as the bonus
  suggests. In the demo, Ravi says other people's requests out loud too: Mei's R25 and the boss's
  R14.
- Is the film sped up? Only the model's wait is trimmed, and the film says so on screen. Our
  real wait on the take: [time it on the take]. No answer was edited.
- Why voice? The track's bonus asks how a dispatcher would really use it: on the floor, on a
  phone. Typing still works everywhere, and the course inbox is still evaluated as text.
- Can a misheard "yes" book something? No. "Book it" only opens the sheet. Saving needs a
  name and the slide, and the preview is recomputed and checked for changes first.
- Does the model see costs or queues? No. It sees the message, the conversation, and the
  workshop, customer and product names. Python computes every number.
- What does memory store? Constraints from confirmed bookings, with who said them and their
  words; a log of answers and bookings; the dispatcher's name and priorities. Nothing is
  learned from the model alone, and a rule can be paused.
- Did you tune on the course seeds? Presets were chosen on development seeds 6000-6029. The
  course seeds were run twice: after the first run a desk case (R12) showed reservation could
  prefer a slightly late shop, so we added an on-time-first charge and re-ran everything. It is
  disclosed in docs/OBJECTIVE_STUDY.md; the default's numbers did not change.
- Where do baselines still win? Under the closure our default is 0.17 points worse on late
  batches than earliest finish. The Sprint 1 lateness preset moves fewer late days under the
  closure than our robustness preset. We do not claim a universal robustness win.
