Our user is Ravi, the production dispatcher. He is on the factory floor, and requests arrive in

SLIDE 3 - The user journey (0:25)
Here is his journey. He holds the microphone and says the order, the date and any constraint.
The assistant answers on screen and out loud: the best workshop, when it arrives, and why.
He says "book it" and slides to confirm. It offers to remember his constraint, and applies it
to that customer's next order. Voice can propose. Only the slide commits.

SLIDE 4 - Architecture and key choices (0:36)
It is one workflow, not a ReAct loop, because every request needs the same checks in order.
Speech becomes text in the browser. Python handles commands like "book it" itself. Everything
else is one structured-output call, and every field must quote the message or Python rejects it.
Deterministic tools then find the order, apply remembered rules, check eligibility, estimate and
rank. Replies are Python templates, so the model never does arithmetic. A person confirms, and
SQLite keeps the record and the memory, as exact rules rather than a vector store.

SLIDE 5 - Progress (0:18)
Here is every feature with its owner. The assistant, memory, the four-objective scorer, the desk

SLIDE 6 - Validation plan (0:33)
For allocation we measure late batches and late days in the course simulator, normal and with a
closure, against the three baselines and earliest finish, choosing settings on separate seeds
first. Our default is 1.4 percent late against 1.7, with 68 percent fewer late days. For
language: all 30 inbox requests by kind, plus held-out rewordings, labelled by two teammates
separately. Hard cases: impossible dates, ineligible workshops, misheard orders. We give
options, refuse with a reason, and read the order back before booking.

Q&A PREPARATION (not read aloud)
- Why voice? The track's bonus asks how a dispatcher would really use it: on the floor, on a
  phone. Typing still works everywhere, and the course inbox is still evaluated as text.
- Can a misheard "yes" book something? No. "Book it" only opens the sheet. Saving needs a
  name and the slide, and the preview is recomputed and checked for changes first.
- Does the model see costs or queues? No. It sees the message, the conversation, and the
  workshop, customer and product names. Python computes every number.
- What does memory store? Constraints from confirmed bookings, with who said them and their
  words; a log of answers and bookings; the dispatcher's name and priorities. Nothing is
  learned from the model alone, and a rule can be paused.
- Did you tune on the course seeds? Presets were chosen on development seeds 6000-6029. The
  course seeds were run twice: after the first run a desk case (R12) showed reservation could
  prefer a slightly late shop, so we added an on-time-first charge and re-ran everything. It is
  disclosed in docs/OBJECTIVE_STUDY.md; the default's numbers did not change.
- Where do baselines still win? Under the closure our default is 0.17 points worse on late
  batches than earliest finish. The Sprint 1 lateness preset moves fewer late days under the
  closure than our robustness preset. We do not claim a universal robustness win.
