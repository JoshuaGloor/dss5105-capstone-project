Before the meeting

  40-minute limit. Everyone joins on a laptop with headphones, from a quiet room.
- Charles starts the app with a fresh plan, so the numbers match this script (a new folder per
  attempt, inside the project):

  In Chrome, open http://localhost:8501, allow the microphone, tap the avatar, enter "Charles",
  turn "Read answers aloud" on, Save. Leave Priorities on Recommended. Stay online. Do Not Disturb.
Recording
1. Cameras off (cleaner), everyone muted except the speaker. Host clicks Record > Record on this
   computer.
2. Slides 1-8: one person shares Presentation_Group11_Sprint2.pptx in Keynote or PowerPoint,
   playing full screen, and advances; each speaker unmutes only for their slides. End on slide
   8 for two seconds, with Niyanthri: "Now, the demo."
3. Stop sharing after slide 8. Pause the recording if you need a moment, then resume.
4. Demo: Charles shares only the Chrome window with the app (not the desktop, not a PDF) and
   ticks "Share sound", so the app's voice is recorded directly. Start straight in the app. Only
   Charles talks. No cuts inside the demo; if it goes wrong, stop, restart the app with a new
   runtime folder and record the demo again. If a rehearsal runs past 2:00, drop the
   (optional) steps.
5. Stop the recording and end the meeting. Zoom converts it to an mp4 in Documents/Zoom; do not
   close Zoom until the conversion finishes.

"We designed the journey for the floor. The dispatcher holds the mic and says the order. The
desk answers on screen and out loud: the best workshop, when it arrives, and why. 'Book it' opens

SLIDE 4 - Architecture (1:08-1:35)  Saadhvika
"This is what we built. Speech becomes text in the browser, and Python answers commands like
'book it' itself. Everything else is one structured-output call, and every field must quote the
message. Deterministic tools then look up the order, apply remembered rules, check eligibility
and rank, so the model never does arithmetic. A slide confirms, and SQLite keeps the booking and
the rule."

SLIDE 5 - Key choices (1:35-1:53)  Saadhvika
"We chose a fixed workflow over a ReAct agent, because every request needs the same checks;
exact lookups over embeddings, so we never fetch the wrong order; exact rules in SQLite over a
vector store; and a slide, never a spoken yes."

Q&A PREPARATION (not read aloud)
- Ruby (Duong Thanh Nhan) cannot attend: Ruby emails Salman and Jack with the reason before the
  session, and slide 1 shows "Absent" with that reason. Any member may be asked about Ruby's desk
  redesign: one answer at a time, objective panel, saved plan, Show working.
- Who is the user? SweaterCo's dispatcher, the person who handles the dispatch chat. Five
  people post requests there (Ravi, Priya, Mei, Chen and the boss). In the demo Charles plays
  the dispatcher and says requests from the chat out loud: Mei's R25 and the boss's R14.
- Why "StitchFlow"? Stitch for garments, flow for orders moving to the right workshop.
  QuickStitch is one of the eight workshops, not us.
- How was the evaluation set built? 205 requests generated from templates with fixed seeds,
  across the seven request kinds: 140 train, 65 test, and the test wording never appears in
  train. Labels come from construction (the generator knows the order, date and constraint it
  wrote) plus the deterministic dispatch preview, so no model labels them. Results are
  behaviour-rubric pass rates from one gpt-5.6 run on intake-v2: train 134/140 (95.7%), test
  63/65 (96.9%). They are not model accuracy. The three original train failures (TR053,
  TR056, TR059) were an unneeded quantity question blocking a refusal, fixed in intake-v3.
- Is the demo real time? Yes. It is one take with no cuts; only the start and end are trimmed.
- Why voice? The track's bonus asks how a dispatcher would really use it: on the floor, on a
  phone. Typing still works everywhere, and the course inbox is still evaluated as text.
- Can a misheard "yes" book something? No. "Book it" only opens the sheet. Saving needs a
  name and the slide, and the preview is recomputed and checked for changes first.
- Does the model see costs or queues? No. It sees the message, the conversation, and the
  workshop, customer and product names. Python computes every number.
- What does memory store? Constraints from confirmed bookings, with who said them and their
  words; a log of answers and bookings; the dispatcher's name and priorities. Nothing is
  learned from the model alone, and a rule can be paused.
- Did you tune on the course seeds? Presets were chosen on development seeds 6000-6029. The
  course seeds were run twice: after the first run a desk case (R12) showed reservation could
  prefer a slightly late shop, so we added an on-time-first charge and re-ran everything. It is
  disclosed in docs/OBJECTIVE_STUDY.md; the default's numbers did not change.
- Where do baselines still win? Under the closure our default is 0.17 points worse on late
  batches than earliest finish. The Sprint 1 lateness preset moves fewer late days under the
  closure than our robustness preset. We do not claim a universal robustness win.
